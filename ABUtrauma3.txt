Complex Trauma
Complex trauma commonly refers to repeated or prolonged exposure to traumatic events, often in situations that are difficult to escape.
Complex PTSD is a specific diagnosis in ICD-11.

Post-Traumatic Growth
Definition
Post-traumatic growth is a positive change in functioning resulting from the struggle to resolve a major life crisis (Calhoun et al., 2000).
In existential psychology, “transformative power of suffering” (Tedeschi & Calhoun, 2004)

Individual Responses and Recovery
Peritraumatic Risk Factors:
Intensity, variety, frequency, and duration of trauma
Severity of the traumatic event and perceived threat to life
Peritraumatic dissociation
In a 2015 study conducted with veterans, it was found that the “change in life philosophy” subscale of the PTG data scored higher than the other subscales. This was thought to be due to the intensity of their confrontation with death. The same study found a strong correlation between levels of cognitive flexibility and post-traumatic growth (Hijazi et al., 2015).

Post-traumatic Risk Factors:

It has been observed that when individuals with a low socioeconomic status are provided with adequate economic resources following a traumatic event, their likelihood of developing PTD increases (Manove et al., 2019).

--- Slide 25 ---
Reactions to Traumatic Events
Between 41% and 86% of the population experiences at least one traumatic event in their lifetime.
However, a large proportion is able to recover without developing any medical or psychopathological disorders (Steudte-Schmiedgen, 2016).
Prevalence rates have also been found to be in the 30–40 percent range among teams providing post-disaster support (Kross et al., 2008).
While prevalence rates were around 20 percent in community screenings conducted after the August 17, 1999, earthquake, they were found to be 63 percent among those who sought treatment (Öztürk MO, 2015).

--- Slide 26 ---
Reactions to Traumatic Events
The development of PTSD following a traumatic experience; depends on factors such as personality traits, social support, and socioeconomic status (Abraido-Lanza et al., 1998).
Some studies indicate a negative correlation between age and PTG (60).
There is evidence that direct exposure to a traumatic event increases the likelihood of PTG. (Shakespeare-Finch & Lurie-Beck, 2014).
The type and nature of the trauma experienced also influence PTG.
2019 meta-analysis found that individuals exposed to occupational trauma and those living with a terminal illness (such as cancer) exhibited higher rates of PTSD compared to those who had been in a traffic accident (Wu et al., 2019).
